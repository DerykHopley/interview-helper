# Example Scenario

---
title: Turned around a team that kept missing its sprint goals
role: Engineering manager
skills: [people leadership, delivery]
measurableResults: [Sprint goals met 5 sprints in a row, after missing 6 of the previous 8]
---

## Situation

I took over a team of six that had missed six of its last eight sprint goals. Two senior engineers had just left,
and the rest of the team was working on five things at once.

## Task

Get the team delivering what it promised again, without burning anyone out.

## Action

I held one-to-ones with everyone in my first week to hear what was in the way. I cut work in progress to two items
at a time, made the sprint goal one sentence the whole team agreed on, and paired each junior with a mid-level
engineer for the hardest tickets.

## Result

The team met its sprint goal five sprints in a row, and nobody on the team left in the following year.

# Example Scenario

---
title: Coached an underperforming engineer into leading a service
role: Engineering manager
skills: [performance management, developing people, feedback]
measurableResults: [Review turnaround from 3 days to under 1 day, Promoted to tech lead within 9 months]
---

## Situation

One engineer on my team was missing reviews and deadlines, and others had started working around him. He had been
on the team for three years and knew the billing service better than anyone.

## Task

Tell him clearly that his work wasn't meeting the bar, and help him get back to it.

## Action

I gave him specific examples from the last month and asked what was going on. He was bored and had stopped caring
about small tickets. We agreed a 60-day plan with weekly check-ins, and I gave him ownership of the billing service's
reliability work, with a clear goal for review turnaround.

## Result

Within two months his review turnaround went from three days to under one. Nine months later he was promoted to tech
lead of the billing service.

# Example Scenario

---
title: Pushed back on a launch date the plan couldn't meet
role: Engineering manager
skills: [stakeholder management, planning]
measurableResults: [Launched 3 weeks later than asked, with no rollback, Two of three planned regions live on day one]
---

## Situation

The operations director announced a launch date for a new picking app across three regions, two months away. My
team's estimate said it would take at least three.

## Task

Change the plan to something the team could deliver, without losing the director's trust.

## Action

I brought the estimate, broken down by feature, and showed which features fitted in two months. I proposed launching
two regions on the original date, with the third three weeks later, and agreed with the director which features
could wait.

## Result

Two regions went live on the original date and the third three weeks later, with no rollback. The director asked me
to plan the next launch the same way.

# Example Scenario

---
title: Hired five engineers in one quarter
role: Engineering manager
skills: [hiring, onboarding]
measurableResults: [5 hires in 12 weeks, First merged change within 5 days of starting]
---

## Situation

My team was given budget for five new engineers, and the company's hiring process took about four months per hire.

## Task

Hire five good engineers within the quarter, and get them productive quickly.

## Action

I wrote a clear role description with the team, replaced the take-home test with a paired exercise on a real past
bug, and trained two senior engineers to interview. I also wrote an onboarding checklist that ended in a small
change shipped in the first week.

## Result

We hired five engineers in twelve weeks. Each one merged their first change within five days of starting.
